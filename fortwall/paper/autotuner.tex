\subsection{Automated Pool-Count Selection}
\label{sec:autotuner}

The clustering of Section~\ref{sec:clustering} consolidates per-script profiles
into $k$ pools, but leaves $k$ as a free operator parameter. Choosing $k$ is a
genuine trade-off: too few pools inflate each script's allow-list toward the
global union, widening the attack surface, whereas too many pools fragment a
fixed worker/memory budget and degrade tail latency. We therefore reframe the
choice of $k$ as a \emph{constrained optimization} that FortWall solves
automatically.

\paragraph{Formulation.}
Let each distinct script profile $c$ (a Stage~2 category) have minimal syscall
set $s_c$ and traffic weight $w_c$, the number of URLs mapped to it. A pooling
into $k$ pools assigns $c$ to a pool whose \emph{effective} policy is the union
of its members restored with the global intersection baseline,
\begin{equation}
P(c) \;=\; \Big(\textstyle\bigcup_{c'\in \mathrm{pool}(c)} s_{c'}\Big) \;\cup\; I,
\qquad P(c)\supseteq s_c,
\end{equation}
where $I$ is the intersection every pool inherits. Reusing the metrics of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics}, we score a pooling with the request-weighted
\emph{attack-surface percentage}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ASP}(k) \;=\; 100 - \mathrm{SP}(k),
\qquad
\mathrm{SP}(k) \;=\; \frac{\sum_c w_c\,\big(\mathrm{TOTAL}-|P(c)|\big)/\mathrm{TOTAL}}{\sum_c w_c}\cdot 100,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ ASP is simply the complement of the blocked-syscall share SP, weighted by
traffic. The worst-case inflation from pooling is measured exactly as
$\Delta\mathrm{SP}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{SN}$ in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathrm{SP}(k)=\max_c \frac{|P(c)\setminus s_c|}{\mathrm{TOTAL}}\cdot 100,
\qquad
\Delta\mathrm{SN}(k)=\max_c \frac{|(P(c)\setminus s_c)\cap \mathrm{SN}|}{\mathrm{SN\_BASE}}\cdot 100 .
\end{equation}
FortWall selects $k$ by
\begin{equation}
\min_{k}\ \mathrm{ASP}(k)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\Delta\mathrm{SN}(k)\le \tau,\ \ k\le K,
\label{eq:autotune}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the pool budget (bounded by memory and worker fragmentation, not by
CPU cores: pools are sets of worker processes, so pool count does not by itself
determine scheduling pressure) and $\tau$ is an optional cap on the worst-case
sensitive-syscall inflation an operator will tolerate.

\paragraph{Solver.}
Minimizing $\sum_j |P_j|$ over $k$-partitions is NP-hard (it is a union-cost /
correlation-clustering objective), so we do not search partitions directly;
instead we treat the agglomerative clustering of Section~\ref{sec:clustering} as
a fixed heuristic solver and tune only its single parameter $k$.
$\mathrm{ASP}(k)$ is monotonically non-increasing in $k$ --- more pools yield
tighter unions --- decreasing from the single-pool global union toward the
Saphire per-script ideal, but with sharply diminishing returns. FortWall sweeps
$k$ over the feasible range, discards points violating the $\tau$ constraint, and
selects the \emph{knee} of the $\mathrm{ASP}(k)$ curve: the smallest $k$ that
captures most of the surface reduction. We detect the knee with the standard
maximum-distance-to-chord (Kneedle) rule, which requires no additional
parameters. Algorithm~\ref{alg:autotune} summarizes the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Auto-tuning the pool count $k$}
\label{alg:autotune}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require profiles $\{(s_c,w_c)\}$, sensitive set $\mathrm{SN}$, budget $K$, cap $\tau$
\Ensure recommended pool count $k^\star$
\State $\mathcal{F}\gets\emptyset$
\For{$k \gets k_{\min}$ \textbf{to} $K$}
  \State build pooling with $k$ pools (dedicated high-share $+$ clustered)
  \State compute $\mathrm{ASP}(k),\ \Delta\mathrm{SN}(k)$ via Eq.~(2)--(4)
  \If{pooling is well-defined \textbf{and} $\Delta\mathrm{SN}(k)\le\tau$}
     \State $\mathcal{F}\gets\mathcal{F}\cup\{k\}$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $k^\star \gets \textsc{Knee}\big(\{(k,\mathrm{ASP}(k)):k\in\mathcal{F}\}\big)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The sweep is offline and inexpensive: each candidate $k$ reuses the existing
Stage~2 pool-planning pass, so tuning costs $O(K)$ clustering runs and no
additional profiling. If the returned $k^\star$ equals $K$, the budget --- rather
than the security objective --- is the binding constraint, which FortWall reports
as a signal to raise $K$.

\subsection{Auto-Tuner Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval-autotuner}

We evaluate the auto-tuner offline on the committed Stage~2 profiles, with pool
budget $K=12$ and no sensitive cap ($\tau=\infty$) unless stated. This isolates
the security objective; the runtime consequences of the selected $k$ are studied
in Section~\ref{sec:eval-autotuner-runtime}.

\paragraph{Selected pool counts.}
Table~\ref{tab:autotune} reports, per application, the pool count $k^\star$ that
FortWall selects and its scores at that point. Across all applications the
auto-tuner settles on a \emph{small} budget ($k^\star\in[5,8]$), well below the
ceiling $K=12$: the security objective, not the budget, is binding in every case.
The knee is pronounced --- expanding to the largest feasible budget reduces ASP
by at most $0.5$ percentage points beyond $k^\star$, confirming that a handful of
pools recovers nearly all of the attack-surface benefit of per-script filtering.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Auto-tuner selection per application ($K=12$, $\tau=\infty$).
$k^\star$ is the selected pool count; SP/ASP/$\Delta$SN follow
Section~\ref{sec:metrics}. ASP $=100-$SP.}
\label{tab:autotune}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Application & $k^\star$ & SP\% & ASP\% & $\Delta$SN\% & budget binding? \\
\midrule
WordPress   & 7 & 83.4 & 16.6 & 45.0 & no \\
Drupal      & 7 & 88.4 & 11.6 & 25.0 & no \\
Joomla      & 8 & 75.5 & 24.5 & 50.0 & no \\

\end{table}

\paragraph{Metric consistency.}
The $\Delta\mathrm{SN}$ values the auto-tuner reports are computed with the same
worst-case definition as Section~\ref{sec:metrics} and agree with the per-pool
$\Delta\mathrm{SN}$ figures reported there (e.g.\ up to $45\%$ on WordPress),
confirming ASP and $\Delta\mathrm{SN}$ are the existing metrics re-expressed, not
new quantities.

\paragraph{Effect of the sensitive constraint.}
Tightening $\tau$ shifts the selection toward larger $k$, which splits pools more
finely and lowers worst-case sensitive inflation. On WordPress, for instance,
imposing $\tau=40\%$ renders the $k^\star=7$ solution infeasible
($\Delta\mathrm{SN}=45\%$) and moves the recommendation to $k=10$
($\Delta\mathrm{SN}=35\%$) at a negligible ASP cost. The knob thus lets an
operator trade a few extra pools for a hard bound on sensitive-syscall exposure.
